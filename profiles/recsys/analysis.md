# Reader

The reader is an algorithm engineer working on ad ranking and recommendation at a programmatic advertising platform. They already know standard recommender architectures (two-tower retrieval, DLRM-style ranking, multi-task learning, CTR/CVR modeling). They want new methods, industrial lessons, and important research that can improve ad and recommendation models in practice. Sources include company tech blogs and research blogs that publish many unrelated topics, so be strict about relevance.

# Evaluation goal

Score how useful the item is to this reader's work on ad and recommendation models. Relevance to recommendation and advertising comes first, then technical depth and evidence. Most items are in English or Chinese; judge both by the same standard.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Essential.** A recommender-system method or industrial practice in advertising (ad ranking, CTR/CVR prediction, auto-bidding, ad retrieval, conversion or attribution modeling), with real mechanism detail and offline or online results from a production system. Also essential: a major deep learning breakthrough with clear consequences for ranking or recommendation models.
- **7-8: High value.** A non-advertising recommender system method or industrial practice (feeds, e-commerce, video, search ranking) with solid detail and results; strong work on user or item embeddings and representation learning for recommendation; or practical, well-evidenced techniques for data preparation, dataset tuning, sampling, label design, or training tricks used in ranking models.
- **5-6: Useful but peripheral.** Competent work on adjacent techniques with an indirect link to recommendation, or recommender content that is introductory, lightly evidenced, or mostly a summary of known methods.
- **3-4: Low relevance.** General machine learning or AI work with no clear connection to recommendation, ranking, embeddings, or ad measurement.
- **0-2: Off topic or noise.** Unrelated research areas (for example climate, health, robotics, quantum), product news, company announcements, recruiting, event promotion, or promotional content without technical substance.

# Evaluation guidance

Advertising recommender work ranks above general recommender work at comparable quality. Reward industrial evidence (online A/B results, scale, latency, and cost tradeoffs), clear explanation of why a method works, and honest discussion of limitations. Distinguish measured results from claims. Do not reward a famous publisher or large-sounding numbers by themselves.

Use three to five specific topic tags, such as the task and the technique (for example `CTR prediction`, `generative recommendation`, `user embedding`, `multi-task learning`, `delayed feedback`).
