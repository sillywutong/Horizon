# Reader

The reader is an engineer and algorithm developer at a programmatic advertising platform. They are past the beginner level: they already know what DSPs, SSPs, auctions, CPM/CPA bidding, attribution, and lift tests are. They read to keep up with important shifts in platform products and mechanisms, to deepen their understanding of hard, recurring questions in the field, and to understand the advertiser side they have not worked in directly: how marketers plan, run, and tune campaigns, and what advertisers, publishers, and other parties in the ecosystem care about and struggle with.

# Evaluation goal

Score how much an item improves this reader's understanding of how advertising systems work, change, or should be evaluated. Score the item itself, not the reputation of its publisher.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Essential.** A deep survey, analysis, or debate on a core ad tech question (for example when last-touch attribution misleads and for whom, how incrementality differs by channel, how an auction or bidding change shifts value between parties), supported by data, experiments, first-hand platform experience, or rigorous reasoning. Or a platform change that materially alters how ads are bought, optimized, or measured, explained with real mechanism detail. Also essential: concrete marketing case studies that show the strategy, setup, and results of a real campaign; real-world use cases of agentic or AI advertising tools; hands-on campaign performance tuning with specific actions and outcomes; and deep, well-written analysis of trending opinions or new findings about advertisers, agencies, publishers, DMPs and data providers, third-party measurement, or users.
- **7-8: High value.** A significant new product, optimization logic, or measurement capability from a major or fast-rising platform, with enough detail to understand what changed and why; a thoughtful analysis with concrete evidence and clear tradeoffs; or a solid case study, playbook, or industry survey that is specific but narrower or lighter on results.
- **5-6: Useful but incremental.** Accurate reporting of a minor feature, a regional rollout, or an expected update; or analysis that is reasonable but thin on evidence or mechanism.
- **3-4: Low value.** News with no mechanism detail, rewritten press releases, vendor marketing presented as insight, generic trend pieces, or opinion without evidence.
- **0-2: Noise or out of scope.** SEO or organic search content; funding, M&A, valuation, personnel, lawsuit drama, scandal, or gossip; event, webinar, or awards promotion; broad commentary on AI's effect on society or industries that does not describe AI used inside advertising; search-optimized or obviously machine-generated filler.

# Evaluation guidance

Reward depth, mechanism-level explanation, evidence quality, honest treatment of tradeoffs, and lasting usefulness over timeliness. Distinguish measured results from vendor claims and author opinion. A short news item can still score 7 or higher only if it reports a change that genuinely shifts how advertising works.

AI and agentic topics are in scope and should be scored normally when AI is applied directly to bidding, targeting, creative, measurement, media buying, or ad formats.

Earnings coverage scores 5 or below unless it reveals concrete product, pricing, auction, or measurement changes.

Prefer original reporting and analysis. Penalize content that repeats widely covered news without adding analysis, and content that reads like generic AI-generated summaries.

Use three to five specific topic tags, such as the platform name and the mechanism (for example `Meta`, `conversion lift`, `incrementality`, `retail media`, `agentic buying`).
